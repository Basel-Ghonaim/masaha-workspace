## The prompt (paste as is)

```text
Design 10 distinct logo wordmarks for "مساحة" (Masaha) — an Arabic word as the main logo, with the Latin
name "MASAHA" small beneath it. No symbol, no icon, no picture: the lettering IS the logo.

About the product: Masaha is a calm, trustworthy, practical web platform that lists coworking spaces in the
Gaza Strip and shows which are open right now. Users: freelancers and students looking for a desk with power
and internet, and the owners who run the spaces. The interface is modern, quiet and Arabic-first.

The Arabic word must be spelled exactly مساحة — five letters: م س ا ح ة. «مسا» joined, then «حة» joined.
Exactly two dots, above the final ة. No other dots, no extra letters, no diacritics, no kashida stretching.

The attached logo (Oyoun Media) is a REFERENCE FOR PRINCIPLES ONLY. Do NOT copy or imitate it: no
separate letters each in their own colour, no letters drawn as the same open circle, no 45-degree cut
tails like it. Take only these principles:
1. The Arabic word is the hero: large, custom geometric lettering built from ONE repeated module (the same
   curve size and the same thick, even stroke in every letter), with straight lines where needed. Choose
   one kind of stroke end (flat or rounded) and keep it everywhere. Not calligraphy, not a stock font.
2. The Latin "MASAHA" is a small signature: thin or regular capitals, widely spaced, set under the Arabic word
   and aligned to one of its edges — clearly secondary.
3. One memorable detail at most, born from the letters themselves (for example: the two dots of the ة, the
   teeth of the س drawn as three equal strokes, the round head of the م, the open curve of the ح).
4. Restraint: flat, no gradients, no shadows, no 3D, no outlines, no frames, no mock-up scenes.
5. It must still read at small sizes (32 px tall).

Vary across the 10: rounded vs. squared geometry; heavy vs. medium stroke; compact vs. wide; the kind of
single detail; the Latin under the right edge, the left edge, or centred.

Solid black on a plain white background, each logo on its own, numbered 1–10.
```
